Triangles in Proportions

When two triangles have the same height, their areas are in the same proportion as their bases.

  • Years 7–9
  • 15–20 min

These triangles are not similar. They can have completely different shapes. What matters is that they have the same height and that one base is a multiple of the other.

The base of Y is 2 times the base of X, so the area of Y is 2 times the area of X.

Why?

Same height, so the base decides

If two triangles have the same height h:

Area of X = ½ × bX × h Area of Y = ½ × bY × h

The ½ and the h are the same in both, so only the bases make the areas different. If the base of Y is 3 times as long, the area of Y is 3 times as big.

Question 1

Triangles X and Z are between the same parallel lines. How does the area of Z compare with the area of X?

Question 1 answers

Solution

B. The triangles are between the same parallel lines, so they have the same perpendicular height. Z has a base of 3 cm, which is half of X’s base of 6 cm. Halving the base while keeping the height the same halves the area.

Question 2

The area of triangle PQR is 30 cm². QR = 12 cm and RT = 6 cm. What is the area of triangle SRT?

Question 2 answers

Solution

B. Both triangles are between the same parallel lines, so they have the same height. The base RT (6 cm) is half of the base QR (12 cm), so the area of SRT is half of 30 cm², which is 15 cm².

Check: 30 = ½ × 12 × h gives h = 5 cm, and ½ × 6 × 5 = 15 cm².

Question 3

The area of triangle PQR is 30 cm². QR = 12 cm and RT = 6 cm. What is the area of the whole triangle PQT?

Question 3 answers

Solution

C. PQT has the same height as PQR. Its base QT = 12 + 6 = 18 cm, which is 1½ times the base QR. So its area is 1½ × 30 = 45 cm².

Another way: PQT is PQR (30 cm²) plus triangle PRT. PRT has base RT = 6 cm, half of QR, so its area is 15 cm². 30 + 15 = 45 cm².

Question 4

D is a point on BC so that BD : DC = 2 : 3. The area of triangle ABC is 50 cm². What is the area of triangle ABD?

Question 4 answers

Solution

A. Triangles ABD and ABC have the same height: the perpendicular distance from A to the line BC.

BD : DC = 2 : 3 means BD is 2 parts out of the 5 parts that make up BC. So BD = ⅖ of BC, and the area of ABD is ⅖ of 50 cm², which is 20 cm².

Question 5

D is a point on BC so that BD = 3 × DC. The area of triangle ADC is 8 cm². What is the area of triangle ABC?

Question 5 answers

Solution

B. ADC and ABC have the same height. BD = 3 × DC, so BC = BD + DC = 4 × DC. The base of ABC is 4 times the base of ADC, so its area is 4 times as big: 4 × 8 = 32 cm².

(24 cm² is the area of ABD, not ABC.)

Question 6

Triangles X and Y are between the same parallel lines. Together their areas add up to 56 cm². What is the area of the larger triangle, Y?

Question 6 answers

Solution

C. The triangles have the same height, so their areas are in the same ratio as their bases: 4 : 10, which simplifies to 2 : 5.

That makes 2 + 5 = 7 equal parts, so one part is 56 ÷ 7 = 8 cm². Triangle X is 2 × 8 = 16 cm² and triangle Y is 5 × 8 = 40 cm².

Question 7

M is the midpoint of BC and N is the midpoint of AM. The area of triangle ABC is 48 cm². What is the area of triangle NBC?

Question 7 answers

Solution

B. Triangles NBC and ABC have the same base BC. N is halfway between A and M, and M is on BC, so N is only half as far from BC as A is. NBC has half the height of ABC.

Same base, half the height, so half the area: ½ × 48 = 24 cm².
