The Josephus Problem

A circle of people. Every second person is eliminated. Where should you stand to be the last one left?

  • Years 7–10
  • 15–30 min
The problem

Survive the circle

An old story tells of Flavius Josephus, a soldier trapped in a cave with 40 companions. Rather than be captured, the group agreed to stand in a circle and eliminate every third person until only one was left. Josephus is said to have worked out where to stand so that he would be the last one alive.

Here is the puzzle.

  1. n people stand in a circle, numbered 1 to n in order.
  2. Counting starts at person 1, who says “1”. The next person says “2”, and so on around the circle.
  3. Whoever says “k” is eliminated and leaves the circle. Counting starts again at the next person still in the circle.
  4. This repeats until only one person is left, the survivor.

We begin with k = 2: every second person goes out.

A small example: n = 5, k = 2

Person 1 says “1” and 2 says “2”, so 2 is out. Then 3 says “1” and 4 says “2”, so 4 is out. Then 5 says “1” and 1 says “2”, so 1 is out. Then 3 says “1” and 5 says “2”, so 5 is out.

Person 3 is the survivor.

Predict first. If 10 people stand in the circle and every second person is eliminated, which position survives? Write your guess down, then use the simulator to test it.

    What do you notice about the survivor when n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32? What happens to the survivor each time n goes up by 1?

    The pattern
    • When n is a power of 2 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32…) the survivor is always person 1.
    • Each extra person after a power of 2 moves the survivor on by 2: 1, 3, 5, 7…
    • The survivor goes back to 1 when n reaches the next power of 2.

    Rule: write n = 2m + L, where 2m is the largest power of 2 that is not more than n. Then the survivor is

    2L + 1

    Shortcut for the current n

    Why it works

    Start with a power of 2, such as 8 people. Going round once removes 2, 4, 6 and 8. Four people are left (1, 3, 5, 7) and person 1 is first to count again. The situation looks the same as before, just with half as many people, so person 1 will be first again, and again, until only person 1 remains.

    Now try n = 2m + L, such as 13 = 8 + 5. The first L eliminations remove people 2, 4, …, 2L. After that, exactly 2m people remain, and the next person to count “1” is person 2L + 1. That is the same as the power-of-2 case, but with person 2L + 1 as the starter. The starter survives.

    Question 1

    There are 16 people in the circle. Who is the survivor?

    Question 1 answers

    Solution

    A. 16 = 24 is a power of 2, so L = 0 and the survivor is 2 × 0 + 1 = 1.

    Question 2

    There are 21 people in the circle. Who is the survivor?

    Question 2 answers

    Solution

    C. 21 = 16 + 5, so L = 5 and the survivor is 2 × 5 + 1 = 11.

    Question 3

    For which of these numbers of people is the survivor person 1?

    Question 3 answers

    Solution

    D. The survivor is 1 only when L = 0, which means n is a power of 2. 64 = 26. The others give 9 (for 20), 3 (for 33) and 33 (for 48).

    Go further

    Challenges

    • The legend has 41 people with k = 3. Use the simulator to find where Josephus should stand.
    • For k = 2, why can the survivor never be an even number?
    • Change k to 3, 4 or 5. Is there still a neat pattern in the survivors?
    Answers
    • Josephus should stand in position 31.
    • The survivor is 2L + 1, which is always odd.
    • For other values of k there is no simple formula like 2L + 1, but the pattern can still be built up step by step from smaller circles.
