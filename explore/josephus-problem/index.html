The Josephus Problem

41 soldiers in a circle. Every second one is struck down. Where should Josephus stand to be the last one left?

  • Years 7–10
  • 15–30 min
The problem

The cave at Jotapata

In 67 CE, during the war between the Jewish people and the Roman Empire, the fortress of Jotapata fell to the Romans. Its commander, Josephus, escaped with 40 soldiers and hid in a cave. The Romans offered to spare them if they surrendered.

His men refused. They would rather die than live as captives, and they called anyone who spoke of surrender a coward. Josephus did not want to die, but he could not simply say so. He proposed that they stand in a circle and take turns: each soldier holding the sword strikes down the next person, then passes the sword on.

There are 41 people in the circle, numbered 1 to 41. Person 1 holds the sword first and strikes down person 2. Josephus is the one who must be left standing at the end. Where should he stand?

Josephus’s own account says the order was drawn by lot. The circle version is a later retelling, and the puzzle has carried his name ever since.

    What do you notice about the survivor when n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32? What happens to the survivor each time n goes up by 1?

    The pattern
    • When n is a power of 2 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32…) the survivor is always person 1.
    • Each extra person after a power of 2 moves the survivor on by 2: 1, 3, 5, 7…
    • The survivor goes back to 1 when n reaches the next power of 2.

    Rule: write n = 2m + L, where 2m is the largest power of 2 that is not more than n. Then the survivor is

    2L + 1

    Shortcut for the current n

    Why it works

    Start with a power of 2, such as 8 people. Going round once removes 2, 4, 6 and 8. Four people are left (1, 3, 5, 7) and person 1 holds the sword again. The situation looks the same as before, just with half as many people, so person 1 will hold the sword first again, and again, until only person 1 remains.

    Now try n = 2m + L, such as 13 = 8 + 5. The first L swings remove people 2, 4, …, 2L. After that, exactly 2m people remain, and the sword is in the hands of person 2L + 1. That is the same as the power-of-2 case, but with person 2L + 1 as the starter. The starter survives.

    Practise

    Use the rule

    Every second person is struck down. Choose an answer, press Check, then test it in the simulator.

    Question 1

    There are 16 people in the circle. Who is the survivor?

    Question 1 answers

    Solution

    A. 16 = 24 is a power of 2, so L = 0 and the survivor is 2 × 0 + 1 = 1.

    Question 2

    There are 21 people in the circle. Who is the survivor?

    Question 2 answers

    Solution

    C. 21 = 16 + 5, so L = 5 and the survivor is 2 × 5 + 1 = 11.

    Question 3

    For which of these numbers of people is the survivor person 1?

    Question 3 answers

    Solution

    D. The survivor is 1 only when L = 0, which means n is a power of 2. 64 = 26. The others give 9 (for 20), 3 (for 33) and 33 (for 48).

    Go further

    Challenges

    • Use the simulator to find where Josephus should stand among the 41 soldiers, then check your answer with the rule.
    • Why can the survivor never be an even number?
    • Where should you stand in a circle of 100 people?
    Answers
    • 41 = 32 + 9, so L = 9 and Josephus should stand in position 2 × 9 + 1 = 19.
    • The survivor is 2L + 1, which is always odd.
    • 100 = 64 + 36, so L = 36 and the survivor is 2 × 36 + 1 = 73.
