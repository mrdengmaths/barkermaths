Same Base, Same Height

Different triangles can look completely different and still have exactly the same area.

Why?

Teacher Notes

Suggested level

Years 7–9

Estimated time

15–25 minutes

Prior knowledge

  • Area of a triangle
  • Base and perpendicular height

Equipment

None

Big idea

Triangles with the same base and the same perpendicular height have equal area.

Common misconception

Students may incorrectly use a sloping side as the height instead of the perpendicular height.

Suggested uses

  • Extension task
  • Early-finisher activity
  • Whole-class investigation
  • Enrichment lesson
  • Discussion / reasoning task

Teacher discussion prompts

  • Where could the top vertex go? Is there any position that would change the area?
  • What would have to change to double the area?
  • When is a sloping side also the perpendicular height?
  • Can you find a triangle with a different base and height but the same area?

NoticeStep 1 of 7

What do you notice about these triangles?

Look carefully. What is the same? What is different?

Hint

Compare their bases and perpendicular heights. (The diagram now shows each base and each perpendicular height.)

TeacherGive students a minute to talk before using the hint. Listen for “same base”, “same height”, “between the same lines” and “they look different but…”. Useful prompts: What is the same? What is different? Which looks biggest, and why?

CalculateStep 2 of 7

Work out the area of each triangle

Remember:

Area = ½ × base × perpendicular height

The three triangles below look very different. Calculate each area, then press Check.

Triangle P

Answer

Area = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm²

The 5 cm side meets the base at a right angle, so it is the perpendicular height. The sloping 9.4 cm side is not used.

Triangle Q

Answer

Area = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm²

The dashed line is the perpendicular height (5 cm). The sloping 6.4 cm sides are not used.

Triangle R

Answer

Area = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm²

The perpendicular height can fall outside the triangle. The sloping sides (5.8 cm and 12.1 cm) are not used.

All three triangles have an area of 20 cm², even though they look so different. What do they have in common?

TeacherAnswer: 20 cm² for each triangle. Watch for students who multiply by a sloping side (for example ½ × 8 × 9.4), or who forget the ½ and answer 40 cm². The sloping lengths are deliberate distractors.

TeacherAsk students to predict the area before they drag C to an extreme position. Challenge them to find positions where the triangle is right-angled (C directly above A or B) and to say why the height is still 5 cm even when C is far away.

ExplainStep 4 of 7

Why does the area stay the same?

  1. The base never changes. It is always 8 cm.
  2. C slides along a line parallel to the base, so its perpendicular distance from the base never changes. The perpendicular height is always 5 cm.
  3. The area formula only uses the base and the perpendicular height:

    A = ½ × b × h

    The position of the top vertex does not appear in it.

The shape changes, but the base and the perpendicular height stay the same, so the area stays the same.

Perpendicular height ≠ sloping side. The perpendicular height makes a right angle with the base. A sloping side is slanted. The area formula uses the perpendicular height, never a sloping side.

Is Sam correct?

Sam says Triangle A must have a greater area because one of its sloping sides is longer.

Is Sam correct? Explain.

Solution

No, Sam is not correct.

Both triangles have a base of 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 5 cm, so each has area ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm².

The longer sloping side (12.1 cm) only shows that the top vertex is further to one side. It is not the perpendicular height. A triangle’s area depends on its base and its perpendicular height, not on the lengths of the other two sides.

TeacherMisconception: using a sloping side as the height. Ask: If the longer side were the height, where would the right angle be? Point out that in Triangle A the height lies outside the triangle.

ChallengeStep 5 of 7

Use the idea

Three reasoning questions, getting harder. Try to use what you have discovered rather than calculating every area.

Challenge 1

Level 1 of 3

Triangle PQR is drawn. P and Q stay where they are.

Which of these triangles must have the same area as triangle PQR? Tick all that apply.

Hint

All four triangles share the base PQ. What do you need to compare to decide whether their areas are equal?

Solution

Triangles PQS and PQT.

PQR, PQS and PQT all have the same base PQ. R, S and T all lie on the line parallel to PQ, so each is the same perpendicular distance from PQ. Same base and same perpendicular height means the same area.

U is closer to PQ, so triangle PQU has a smaller height and a smaller area. V is further from PQ, so triangle PQV has a greater height and a greater area.

Challenge 2

Level 2 of 3

Triangles X, Y and Z all lie between the same pair of parallel lines.

  1. Without calculating either area, explain why triangles X and Y have the same area.
  2. Triangle Z has a base of 3 cm. How does its area compare with the area of triangle X? Explain.
Hint

What is the same about the bases of X and Y? What is the same about their heights?

Solution

(a) X and Y both have a base of 6 cm. They are between the same parallel lines, so the perpendicular distance from the top vertex to the base is the same for both. Same base and same perpendicular height means the same area.

(b) Z has the same perpendicular height as X, but its base is half as long (3 cm compared with 6 cm). Since area = ½ × base × height, halving the base while keeping the height halves the area. Triangle Z has half the area of triangle X.

Challenge 3

Level 3 of 3

The area of triangle PQR is 30 cm². QR = 12 cm and RT = 6 cm.

  1. Find the perpendicular distance between the two parallel lines.
  2. Find the area of triangle SRT.
  3. Find the area of triangle PQT.

For (b) and (c), try to explain how you could find the area without first finding the height.

Hint

Triangles PQR, SRT and PQT all lie between the same parallel lines, so they all have the same perpendicular height. How does each base compare with QR?

Solution

(a) 30 = ½ × 12 × h, so 30 = 6h and h = 5 cm.

(b) Triangle SRT has base RT = 6 cm and the same perpendicular height, 5 cm. Area = ½ × 6 × 5 = 15 cm².
Without finding h: RT is half of QR and the height is the same, so the area is half of 30 cm², which is 15 cm².

(c) Triangle PQT has base QT = 12 + 6 = 18 cm and the same height. Area = ½ × 18 × 5 = 45 cm².
Without finding h: QT is 1½ times QR, so the area is 1½ × 30 = 45 cm².

Final challengeStep 6 of 7

Where is P?

ABCD is a parallelogram with an area of 48 cm². P is a point somewhere on side DC.

  1. Find the area of triangle ABP.
  2. Find the total area of the two unshaded triangles, ADP and BCP.

You are not told where P is, and you are not given the base or height of the parallelogram.

Hint 1

You are not told where P is. Could that mean the answer does not depend on where P is? Think about the base and the height of the parallelogram.

Hint 2

Slide P along DC and watch triangle ABP. What stays the same about it, and what does it share with the parallelogram?

Use the slider (or arrow keys) to move P.

Solution

Let the parallelogram have base AB and perpendicular height h (the distance between AB and DC). Then AB × h = 48.

(a) Triangle ABP has the same base AB. Its top vertex P is on DC, which is parallel to AB, so its perpendicular height is also h, wherever P is. Its area is ½ × AB × h = ½ × 48 = 24 cm².

(b) The two unshaded triangles fill the rest of the parallelogram: 48 − 24 = 24 cm².

Moving P along DC changes the shape of the triangles but never changes the area of triangle ABP. The diagram now shows the shared base and the shared height h.

TeacherThe “aha” is that P’s position is irrelevant: the triangle is always half the parallelogram (same base, same height). Students who try to pick a position for P usually get 24 cm² and then realise any position gives the same result. Extension: what if P is moved off DC, onto a line parallel to DC but further away?

DiscoverStep 7 of 7

What did you discover?

same base
+
same perpendicular height
→
same area
  • If triangles have the same base and the same perpendicular height, they have the same area.
  • Moving the third vertex parallel to the base changes the shape of the triangle but does not change its area.
  • Area depends on the base and the perpendicular height, never on the lengths of the sloping sides.
