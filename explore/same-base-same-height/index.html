Same Base, Same Height

Different triangles can look completely different and still have exactly the same area. Why?

Why?

The shape changes, but the area does not

  1. The base never changes. It is always 8 cm.
  2. C slides along a line parallel to the base, so the perpendicular height is always 5 cm.
  3. The area formula only uses the base and the perpendicular height. Where the top vertex is does not matter. A = ½ × b × h

Perpendicular height ≠ sloping side. The perpendicular height makes a right angle with the base. A sloping side is slanted, and it is never used in the area formula.

Question 1

Sam looks at the two triangles. Which statement is correct?

Question 1 answers

Solution

C. Both triangles have a base of 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 5 cm, so each has area ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm².

The longer sloping side (12.1 cm) is not the perpendicular height. It only shows that the top vertex is further to one side. (Answer D forgets the ½.)

Question 2

Triangle PQR has an area of 20 cm². Which other triangle must also have an area of 20 cm²?

Question 2 answers

Solution

C. Triangles PQR and PQS share the base PQ. R and S are both on the line parallel to PQ, so they are the same perpendicular distance from PQ. Same base and same height means the same area.

U is closer to PQ, so triangle PQU has a smaller height and a smaller area. V is further from PQ, so triangle PQV has a greater height and a greater area.

Question 3

Triangles X and Y lie between the same parallel lines and each has a base of 6 cm. Triangle X has an area of 21 cm². What is the area of triangle Y?

Question 3 answers

Solution

B. X and Y have equal bases (6 cm). Because they are between the same parallel lines, they also have equal perpendicular heights. Equal bases and equal heights mean equal areas, so Y also has an area of 21 cm².

The sloping sides are not needed. (The height here is 2 × 21 ÷ 6 = 7 cm, but we never had to find it.)

Question 4

P is a point so that triangle ABP has an area of 12 square units. Which of these could be the coordinates of P?

Question 4 answers

Solution

C. The base AB is 4 units long. For an area of 12, ½ × 4 × h = 12, so the perpendicular height h must be 6 units.

AB is horizontal, so P must be 6 units above it, on the line y = 1 + 6 = 7. Only (0, 7) is on that line. It does not matter how far left or right P is. (The others have heights 3, 5 and 4, giving areas 6, 10 and 8.)

Question 5

ABCD is a rectangle, 12 cm by 5 cm. P is a point somewhere on side DC. What is the area of triangle ABP?

Question 5 answers

Solution

B. Triangle ABP has base AB = 12 cm. P is on DC, which is parallel to AB, so the perpendicular height of the triangle is the distance between AB and DC: 5 cm. This is true wherever P is.

Area = ½ × 12 × 5 = 30 cm², which is exactly half of the rectangle.

Question 6

ABCD is a parallelogram with an area of 80 cm². P is a point somewhere on side DC. What is the total area of the two shaded triangles, ADP and BCP?

Question 6 answers

Solution

B. Let the parallelogram have base AB and perpendicular height h, so AB × h = 80.

Triangle ABP has the same base AB. P is on DC, which is parallel to AB, so its height is also h wherever P is. Its area is ½ × AB × h = ½ × 80 = 40 cm².

The two shaded triangles make up the rest of the parallelogram: 80 − 40 = 40 cm².

Question 7

ABCD is a trapezium with AB parallel to DC. The diagonals AC and BD cross at E. Which pair of triangles must have equal areas?

Question 7 answers

Solution

C. Triangles ABD and ABC have the same base AB. D and C are both on the line parallel to AB, so the two triangles have the same height and the same area.

Both of these triangles contain triangle ABE. Remove ABE from each and the leftover pieces must be equal: triangle ADE and triangle BCE have equal areas.

Question 8

ABCD is a parallelogram with an area of 60 cm². P is a point somewhere inside it. Triangle ABP has an area of 11 cm². What is the area of triangle CDP?

Question 8 answers

Solution

B. Let the parallelogram have base AB (= DC) and height h. P is h₁ above AB and h₂ below DC, so h₁ + h₂ = h.

Area of ABP = ½ × AB × h₁ and area of CDP = ½ × DC × h₂ = ½ × AB × h₂. Together: ½ × AB × (h₁ + h₂) = ½ × AB × h = ½ × 60 = 30 cm², wherever P is.

So CDP = 30 − 11 = 19 cm².
